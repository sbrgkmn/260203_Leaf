# Brady-inspired structural series

Source: Ronald H. Brady, *Form and Cause in Goethe’s Morphology*, [Figure 4](https://www.natureinstitute.org/ronald-h-brady/form-and-cause-in-goethes-morphology). The nine **Ranunculus acris** leaves run from bottom left, upward, then down to bottom right. These are successive leaves along a stem, not observed growth stages of one leaf.

The active series interprets fullness, branching, division and terminal simplification through a shared symmetric Buttercup E/C recipe. It does not fit the irregular source silhouettes. `serial-structures.mjs` defines nine authored anchors using eight controls: shoot expansion, shoot contraction, shoot stopping length, blade expansion, blade contraction, blade stopping length, positive rounding weight and negative rounding weight. All retain three shoot cycles and three blade cycles. Left/right position and intensity are exactly equal. Local blade stem contraction is zero to keep blade bases joined.

The default twelve columns sample this nine-anchor path evenly; eight columns and the nine anchors themselves are also available. Numeric settings interpolate linearly between adjacent anchors. Every intermediate is regenerated through the original E/C engine. Discrete edge eligibility can change at a stopping threshold, so smooth parameter interpolation does not guarantee continuous branching topology. Each column contains twelve actual operations; dormant operations may repeat geometry.

The default sequence increases the number of boundary poles through the middle, then reduces to three main shoots at the end. Tests check bilateral symmetry at every construction state, finite geometry, rising and falling complexity, and non-self-intersecting final boundaries for all twelve default forms. These checks do not establish botanical accuracy or guarantee valid geometry for arbitrary edits. The horizontal axis is a computational interpretation of serial leaf variation; the vertical “embryogenesis” axis is E/C construction, not a biological embryo simulation.

`data/brady-reference.mjs` contains silhouettes digitized from the source drawing. Thresholding isolates nine connected components, retains internal holes and simplifies contours by 0.65 source pixels. Manually selected root/tip landmarks register each axis. References are normalized to axis height 10; original drawing heights are retained separately for optional relative display size. These are drawing proportions, not plant measurements. Extraction is reproducible with `python tools/extract-brady-series.py` (requests, numpy, opencv-python).

The reference strip is folded below the main chart. Its asymmetry remains visible as source evidence, but never enters the generated geometry. The transition inspector compares adjacent symmetric structural anchors and their generated intermediate. Earlier silhouette fits in `data/brady-fits.mjs` and offline fitting tools remain archived; the app no longer loads those fits or presents their scores as validation of this series.

## Blade surface lesson

The primary lesson is `blade-node-study.mjs`. Its construction strip runs a controlled linear E/C recipe through two cycles, stopping after the second contraction (15 recursive points, five shoots). Curve controls are introduced afterwards. These layouts are authored post-C2 surface studies, not extra E/C operations or a claim that the original engine implies these finishes.

All four treatments use four quadratic CVs per half-leaf, mirrored into a continuous blade with a natural tapered base. 01 is convex and rounded; 02 keeps pointed upper tips; 03 follows the supplied half-leaf sketch with one lateral pair and an anchored sinus; 04 rounds the contracted profile. Smooth joins use the midpoint of adjacent controls, giving identical quadratic derivatives. In 03 this removes the kink between CVs 3 and 4. The diagram shows only the right-half construction with a bold contour and pale control polygon. The top strip retains real E/C history separately from these authored surface finishes. Tests check smooth joins, mirroring, continuity, finite geometry and absence of crossings.

`blade-configurations.mjs` reduces Buttercup to two shoot cycles and no blade cycles: E/C/E/C establishes five positive poles. Four finishes independently pair the upper-tip and lower-node treatments: C/C rounded/rounded, E/C sharp/rounded, C/E rounded/sharp, and E/E sharp/sharp. All share identical control points and recursion. In C/E, two cubic arcs span the full lobe between exact lower nodes, meeting with a continuous tangent at the upper crest. This retains lobe height while rounding and filling its flanks. Adjoining node arcs collapse to those anchors to avoid overlapping segments. These E/C pair labels describe drawing treatments, not a change to the alternating growth operations. Existing saved recipes and lab samples retain their drawing behavior.

The comparison and development plates reuse the Worked buttercup construction renderer, including inherited axes, pole markers and previous boundaries. Outline/fill views and two SVG exports support inspection. Earlier signed-bow and margin-depth studies remain in the source but are no longer the active Blade surfaces lesson.
