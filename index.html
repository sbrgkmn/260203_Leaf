How development works

1 / Shoots. E anchors a rotated vector on the current vein axis. C inserts a negative point on each flank and moves it toward the incoming origin. Radial or linear continuation selects which descendants keep developing; other edges rest. The first E position sets the initial branching origin along the stalk.

2 / Blades. When an individual shoot edge reaches its depth or length limit, it starts blade recursion locally. Endpoint polarity determines which blade edges continue. Position and intensity vary by generation using the curves saved in the original Grasshopper files.

Boundary rounding. Positive and negative poles have separate rational Bézier weights. These soften tips and indentations without changing recursion. The optional overlay shows continuing edges in green and resting edges in gray.

Parameters and rule variants were recovered from the supplied Grasshopper definitions. These saved files may differ from the publication revision. Source rules are retained, including their individual parameter limits and stem conditions; extreme edits can cross the boundary. Edits persist per leaf while the page stays open.
