How development works

1 / Shoots. Choose a connected blade or separate leaflets, then establish the branch arrangement. Base, middle, and tip profiles control shoot length; spacing and rotation control their distribution. E introduces shoots; C separates the surface around them.

2 / Blades. Signed E/C displacements develop local surfaces around the retained framework. Primary tips and attachment points stay fixed. Compound leaves broaden their individual leaflets; fern recipes add secondary pinnae.

3 / Margins. Local subdivision adds smaller lobes and teeth. A cycle schedule reduces intensity over time, and an affected-region control can focus the operation on upper shoots, lower shoots, or their tips.

Positive E moves out; negative E moves in. Positive C draws toward the local shoot axis; negative C moves away. The initial E establishes the framework, so its opposite sign is an equivalent mirrored seed. Zero initial E produces no surface. Local displacements are reduced when needed to avoid a folded boundary. Rounding is a drawing control.

These developmental recipes interpret Sabri Gokmen, Metamorphic Leaves, Leonardo 53(5), 2020, figures 3–7. They extend the published method; they do not recover its original parameters. Edits are retained separately for each leaf while this page stays open.
